# About this document

Third-party views of Ryanair: consumer-group research, a satisfaction survey, press reports and commentary on the brand's social media. It is opinion and survey data, not company fact; the product mechanics themselves are in the primary document fares_and_extras.md. Which? is a consumer advocacy group with a position, and its survey is not weighted or representative, so treat its numbers as a measure of mood. Sources are in sources.md.

# The shared joke: booking feels like dodging traps

The common view is that the advertised fare is the bait, and the booking flow is a run of traps to step around. It matches what consumer researchers found:

- Which? (February 2026) lists rules for booking Ryanair without overpaying. A "Regular is ideal for your trip!" banner appears when you pick the cheapest fare. Family Plus is shown as "recommended for you". Travel insurance is offered. A pre-ticked box converts euro fares at Ryanair's own exchange rate, which Which? found cost one family £81 more.
- In 15 checked flights, the recommended Regular bundle was cheaper than buying the same extras separately only once.
- Which?'s February 2026 survey says the cost of cabin bags is often not shown until late in booking. One Ryanair customer said: "It is billed as a budget airline but they make money from extras which far outweigh price savings on other flight companies."
- Ryanair's position is that bundles cost the same as the extras bought separately and that its currency rate is competitive.

# Bags and the gate

- The bag sizer at the gate is the most recognisable Ryanair moment. Bags that fail cost £46 to £75 at the gate (Which?, February 2026), wheels and handles included. Which? reported one family charged £400 across return flights.
- Gate staff earn a bonus per oversized bag caught: €1.50, announced in July 2025 to rise to €2.50. Michael O'Leary said around 200,000 passengers a year, about 0.1%, are caught.
- Consumer surveys show most passengers think a cabin bag should be included in the fare. See customer_feedback.md.

# Seats and priority boarding

- On the cheapest fare the seat is random, and the joke is that random means the middle seat, away from your travel companion. In Which?'s survey only 62% of Ryanair passengers sat next to their companion without paying, against over 90% on British Airways, easyJet and Jet2. Ryanair says it does not split passengers more than other airlines do.
- Priority boarding is paid, and it comes bundled with the 10kg overhead bag. Passengers pay to queue first, and pay mainly to keep a normal-sized bag with them. Ryanair's own TikTok joked: "We're Ryanair, your bag's a priority, not you."

# Overall ratings

- Which? airline survey, published 27 February 2026: Ryanair was the worst-rated short-haul airline, scoring 55%. It got two stars for booking, boarding, customer service and cabin, one star for seat comfort, and three stars for value for money. 63% of Ryanair journeys had no problems, against 76% for Jet2.
- Ryanair's response: "Neither we nor our 208m passengers pay any attention to these made-up manufactured surveys or their fake results."

# "Never again", then booking again

- Many people complain about flying Ryanair and then book it again because it is cheap. Adventure.com (April 2024) puts it as "How many times have you said 'Never again' after flying with Ryanair?" and argues that the social media turns that frustration into loyalty.
- Ryanair carried 208.4 million passengers in the year to March 2026, so the complaints and the bookings exist side by side.

# How the brand's social accounts use this

- Commentators describe the social strategy as taking the criticism and making it the content. Complaints become memes and fees become TikToks. Ryanair's social team describes leaning into how people already talk about the brand (see brand_voice.md).
- Its own TikTok, quoted by Adventure.com: "We're Ryanair, of course we're randomly going to allocate your seat."
- One 2026 commentary (PPC Land) argued the voice may soften as EU rules on cabin bags tighten. That is one outlet's opinion.

# What this means for content

- The audience already knows the jokes: the bag sizer, the small personal bag, the random middle seat, paying to board first, paying for water, the €55 airport check-in, "never again". A reply or meme can name the trap instead of denying it. Owning the joke is the brand voice.
- Never deny the reputation with claims like "no hidden fees", "everything included" or "we never split families". Never claim a ranking or satisfaction score the sources do not support.
- Joke about the rule, the bag or the brand, never about a passenger who has just lost money. Someone who paid a gate fee or missed a flight is a person in distress under the guardrails.
- Fees and bag rules are under regulatory pressure (see market_trends.md). Do not present a legal dispute as settled.

# Gaps

- There are no Ryanair-owned customer satisfaction figures besides its in-app "Rate My Flight" survey, which was not read.
- There is no systematic sample of real complaint posts, and no review-site rating was verified directly.
- Ryanair's TikTok lines are quoted by a third party, not taken from the platform.
