# About this document

Competitor and market context only; Ryanair's own facts are in `knowledge_base/primary/`. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md). Mostly trade-press summaries of company results, so verify against the official releases before quoting a number.

# easyJet

- **Scale:** About 190 aircraft in mid-2025. Stated aim of roughly 350 aircraft by 2030 and larger aircraft ("upgauging") to raise capacity per flight without proportional crew cost.
- **Results, year to 30 Sept 2025:** Headline profit before tax £665m (+9%); headline EBIT £703m (+18%). The airline earned £415m and easyJet holidays £250m. A new holidays target of £450m PBT by FY2030. Dividend 13.2p proposed. Q1 FY26 passengers up 7% to 22.7m at a 90% load factor.
- **Positioning:** Combines flights with package holidays; the holidays arm is now over a third of profit. This is the main strategic difference from a pure point-to-point model.
- **Social style:** A 2025 master's thesis (Ryanair, Vueling, easyJet) found easyJet leans on promotional and cultural storytelling rather than humour, and that Instagram Reels are its strongest visibility driver. Earlier commentary describes its X voice as friendly and personal.
- **Gap:** Full-year FY25 passenger total not confirmed in the sources found.

# Wizz Air

- **Scale and growth:** 69.7m passengers in the year to 31 March 2026, up 10% from 63.4m; load factor 90.7%. Rose from tenth to fourth largest European carrier by seats in five years (trade press).
- **Results FY2026:** Revenue €5,691m (+8%), but net profit collapsed to €1.3m from €213.9m. Maintenance costs rose 40%. Engine groundings (Pratt & Whitney GTF) cost capacity: 30 aircraft grounded at 31 March 2026, expected fully back by end-2027. Exited an Abu Dhabi venture and is closing its Vienna base.
- **Positioning:** Central and Eastern Europe focus; ultra-low-cost model closest to Ryanair's.
- **Social style:** No reliable source found. Do not describe Wizz Air's tone in content.

# Where the competitors stand on the hand-luggage issue

All three of easyJet, Wizz Air and Ryanair are named in the 2025 BEUC complaint. A Which? study found that the lowest advertised cabin-bag fees (from £5.99) appeared on under 1% of ~1,500 fares checked across Ryanair, easyJet and Wizz Air.

# Content implications

- **Contrast with easyJet:** Promotional storytelling and a holidays upsell; a post for Ryanair should take its voice from the primary brand guidelines, not copy this.
- **Contrast with Wizz Air:** Wizz Air's profit squeeze from groundings is a weakness, but do not mock a competitor's financial trouble in a post without sign-off from the social team.
- **Safe rule:** Compare on verifiable public facts only, and have a human check any named-competitor claim before it is published.
