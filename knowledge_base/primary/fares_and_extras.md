# Ryanair fares, bags and paid extras

Retrieved on 23 October 2026. Ryanair's help-centre pages block automated reading, so their wording comes from search-result excerpts of those pages. Prices are dynamic, so they change by route, date and market. A price below is an example from a dated source, never a current price to quote. Sources are listed at the end of this document.

## How the product works

The headline fare buys a seat on the plane and one small bag. Almost everything else is a paid extra: a bigger cabin bag, boarding first, choosing a seat, a checked bag, checking in at the airport, food and drink, and insurance. In the year to March 2026 the average fare was €50.60, and ancillary (extras) revenue was €24 per passenger, €4.99bn in total, about a third of group revenue.

## Fare bundles

- **Basic:** the cheapest fare. One small personal bag that must fit under the seat in front, and a random seat allocated at online check-in, which opens 24 hours before departure.
- **Regular:** adds a standard seat, priority boarding and a 10kg overhead cabin bag.
- **Plus:** adds a standard seat and a 20kg checked bag.
- **Flexi Plus:** priority boarding, both cabin bags, a reserved seat, Fast Track where the airport offers it, and date, time or route changes without a change fee (the passenger still pays any fare difference).
- **Family Plus:** offered only on bookings with at least one child or teen.

Ryanair says bundles cost the same as the underlying fare plus the same extras bought separately, because extras are "dynamically priced". Which? priced 15 flights in February 2026 and found the Regular bundle cheaper than Basic plus extras only once.

## Bags

- **Free with every fare:** one small personal bag, 40 × 30 × 20 cm, under the seat in front. Older guides still give the previous 40 × 20 × 25 cm size.
- **10kg overhead cabin bag (55 × 40 × 20 cm):** sold as "Priority & 2 Cabin Bags", or included in Regular and Flexi Plus. The overhead bag and priority boarding are one product: you cannot buy one without the other.
- **Checked bag:** paid, typically 20kg, included in Plus.
- **At the gate:** a bag that fails the size check is charged a fee. In February 2026 Which? reported £46 to £60 for a bag on a Basic fare and £70 to £75 for an overhead bag over 55 × 40 × 20 cm. Wheels and handles count in the measurement.
- Gate staff receive a bonus for each oversized bag they catch: €1.50 per bag, and in July 2025 Ryanair announced it would rise to €2.50 from November 2025, with the monthly cap removed. The increase had not been confirmed when this was retrieved.

## Boarding

- Priority boarding means boarding first through the Priority queue at the gate. It is sold with the 10kg overhead bag, as above. Third-party guides quote roughly €12 to €36 per flight at booking, varying by route and date.
- Since 12 November 2025 boarding passes are digital only, in the Ryanair app after online check-in. Printed passes are no longer accepted.
- A passenger who does not check in online pays an airport check-in fee per passenger per flight: €55 in most of the EU and the UK, €40 in Austria and €30 in Spain, as reported in November 2025. A passenger who checked in online but cannot show the pass on a phone gets a free one at the airport.

## Seats

- Basic fares get a random seat at check-in. Choosing a seat is paid; Which? reported standard seats from £10 and extra legroom up to £20 each way in February 2026.
- Families: an adult travelling with children under 12 (excluding infants) must reserve a seat. Up to four children per adult on the same booking then get a reserved seat free. Infants sit on an adult's lap.

## Onboard and at booking

- Food and drink are not included in any fare. Everything onboard is bought from a paid menu, the cabin is cashless, and orders can be placed in the app. Passengers may bring their own food and soft drinks.
- Booking offers travel insurance. Which? also reported a pre-ticked box that converts euro fares at Ryanair's own exchange rate; Ryanair says its conversion rate is competitive.

## What this means for content

- These are the real mechanics behind most passenger jokes about Ryanair: the bag sizer, the random seat, paying to board first, paying for water. Content can be self-aware about them. It is part of the documented brand voice in brand_voice.md.
- Never state a price, fee amount or bag size that the request does not supply or that this document does not date. Fees change by route and date.
- Never claim something is free, included or "no hidden fees" beyond what is listed here as free: the small personal bag, and children's reserved seats under the family rule.
- Never tell passengers how to dodge a fee or the bag check. The guardrails forbid encouraging people to bypass airline rules.
