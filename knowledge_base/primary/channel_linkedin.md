# LinkedIn channel profile

Team editorial direction for LinkedIn drafts. It applies the platform cues in [brand_voice.md](brand_voice.md) and the examples in [past_content/examples_linkedin.md](past_content/examples_linkedin.md). It is guidance on how to write, not a company fact.

## Tone

Clear, confident and human. Light humour is welcome now and then, but never the chaotic consumer-social tone of X or Instagram.

## Format

A concise hook, short paragraphs, and explicit company, people, route, investment, technology or operational context when it is supplied.

## Post

- Open with a one-line hook; an all-caps headline is fine for news, as on Ryanair's own LinkedIn page.
- Then stack concrete proof points (routes, jobs, aircraft, investment, dates) as short lines or a short list, only when they are supplied.
- A few emoji as line markers are fine. No more than three hashtags.
- Close with a next step (book, apply, read more) only when the request supplies a link or offer; otherwise end on the news itself.

## Press release

- Write it as a Ryanair corporate news release: an all-caps headline stating the news, then three or four short factual paragraphs (what, where, when, why it matters).
- Then one attributed quote. Never invent the quote or the speaker: if the request does not supply them, write `[SPEAKER AND QUOTE: to be supplied by Ryanair Comms]`.
- If the request gives only one or two facts, the release is short. Never pad it with routes, frequencies, jobs, investment or community impact that the request does not state.
- End with a line that reads `ENDS`.
