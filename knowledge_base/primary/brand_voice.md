# Social voice

## Core voice

The documented social voice is a combination of:

- clear and concise wording;
- direct, honest communication;
- playful, self-deprecating humour;
- confidence in the low-cost brand position;
- irreverence and topicality when there is a defensible connection to travel, flying, routes, or the customer experience.
- consumer-social copy can be deliberately blunt, provocative, lowercase and grammatically loose;
- the joke should land quickly: prefer one sharp sentence over an explanation or a polite paragraph;
- do not use emojis, hashtags, corporate filler or automatic apologies in consumer-social copy.

These are working principles, not permission to manufacture insults or controversial claims, target protected groups, threaten people, or invent facts. The social-marketing interview says the team leans into how people already talk about the brand, uses humour and self-deprecation, and links topical conversation back to what Ryanair does.

## Platform cues from the public sample

The same core voice should adapt to the channel:

| Platform | Use this emphasis | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | Lowercase, visual-first, one recognisable travel or flight moment, one short punchline; aim for 3–12 words when a caption can carry it | Dense corporate explanation, polished ad copy, emojis, hashtags, or a joke that needs a paragraph of context |
| X / Twitter | Lowercase, fast, direct and topical; answer in one sharp sentence, ideally 3–12 words, with a clear link to Ryanair or the passenger experience | Long explanations, automatic apologies, emojis, hashtags, treating an unverified rumour as fact, or attacking a protected group |
| LinkedIn | More explicit company, people, investment, route, jobs, technology, or operational context; still human and occasionally playful, but easier to scan | Copying the most chaotic consumer-social tone into an employer or corporate update | No emojis |
| Internal Slack | Direct, practical and collaborative; use the brand voice as context while prioritising clarity for colleagues and a clear next action | Post should still be informative and educating our employees on the new policies or updates |

These channel cues are editorial defaults for the MVP. They must be checked against the actual requested channel and source material for each draft.

## Voice checklist

Before approval, a draft should be:

1. clear enough to understand on first read;
2. concise enough for the requested platform;
3. recognisably connected to travel, routes, fares, airports, or the customer experience;
4. playful or self-aware where the subject makes that safe;
5. specific rather than generic “inspirational” airline copy;
6. free of invented company facts, fabricated quotes, or unverified current events.
