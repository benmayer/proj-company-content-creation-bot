# Social voice

## Core voice

The documented social voice is a combination of:

- clear and concise wording;
- direct, honest communication;
- playful, self-deprecating humour;
- confidence in the low-cost brand position;
- irreverence and topicality when there is a defensible connection to travel, flying, routes, or the customer experience.

These are working principles, not permission to manufacture insults or controversial claims. The social-marketing interview says the team leans into how people already talk about the brand, uses humour and self-deprecation, and links topical conversation back to what Ryanair does.

## Platform cues from the public sample

The same core voice should adapt to the channel:

| Platform | Use this emphasis | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | Short visual-first caption, meme-friendly setup, one recognisable travel or flight moment, concise punchline, restrained emoji use where it adds meaning | Dense corporate explanation or a joke that needs a paragraph of context |
| X / Twitter | Fast reaction, direct reply, topical observation, compact wording, and a clear link to Ryanair or the passenger experience | Treating an unverified rumour or a developing event as fact |
| LinkedIn | More explicit company, people, investment, route, jobs, technology, or operational context; still human and occasionally playful, but easier to scan | Copying the most chaotic consumer-social tone into an employer or corporate update |
| Internal Slack | Direct, practical and collaborative; use the brand voice as context while prioritising clarity for colleagues and a clear next action | Treating an internal draft as approved public copy or hiding an unresolved claim in a joke |

These channel cues are editorial defaults for the MVP. They must be checked against the actual requested channel and source material for each draft.

## Voice checklist

Before approval, a draft should be:

1. clear enough to understand on first read;
2. concise enough for the requested platform;
3. recognisably connected to travel, routes, fares, airports, or the customer experience;
4. playful or self-aware where the subject makes that safe;
5. specific rather than generic “inspirational” airline copy;
6. free of invented company facts, fabricated quotes, or unverified current events.
