# Public-content guardrails

## Public context

Ryanair’s public-facing communication can be topical, blunt, and provocative. That makes current events, public criticism, celebrity references, rumours, and controversies possible creative inputs, but not safe facts by default.

The [Advertising Standards Authority for Ireland case concerning a Ryanair social advertisement](https://adstandards.ie/complaint/airline-services/) records that an advertisement appeared on Ryanair’s official Facebook and Twitter accounts and that complaints raised issues including alcohol-related presentation and suitability for a young audience. This is evidence that social creativity remains subject to advertising standards and audience-suitability constraints; it is not a general statement that every Ryanair post breaches those standards.

## Guardrails for the generator

- Verify current events, public figures, legal matters, controversies, and rumours in a current source before using them. Secondary research belongs in `knowledge_base/secondary/`.
- Do not state allegations, court outcomes, regulatory findings, customer claims, or competitor claims as facts unless the selected source supports the exact wording.
- Do not target protected characteristics, vulnerable people, bereaved people, minors, or individuals in distress for a joke.
- Do not encourage unsafe behaviour, bypassing airport or airline rules, or misrepresenting fare conditions.
- Keep humour directed at a recognisable travel situation, the brand’s own low-cost reality, or a harmless cultural moment—not at a private individual.
- Escalate drafts involving tragedy, war, disasters, serious accidents, active legal disputes, or unverified celebrity gossip for human review; do not auto-publish.
- Keep commercial claims and disclaimers aligned with the dated source, market, currency, travel dates, and booking channel.

## Review questions

1. Is the topical hook verified and still current?
2. Is the joke understandable without humiliating someone or exploiting harm?
3. Could a reasonable reader mistake a joke, rumour, or estimate for an official company fact?
4. Are fare, route, date, and availability claims traceable?
5. Does the platform change the level of context and professionalism required?
