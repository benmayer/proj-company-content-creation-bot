# Ryanair on LinkedIn: examples

Collected on 23 October 2026. Headlines are verbatim; the body structure is described rather than copied. Any figure inside an example was true only on its date and is not a current company fact.

## Press release

Real headlines from [Ryanair corporate news](https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/):

- "RYANAIR TO CARRY ALMOST 700,000 PASSENGERS THROUGH IRISH AIRPORTS THIS OCTOBER MIDTERM" (23 Oct 2026)
- "RYANAIR MARKS 15 MILLION PASSENGERS THROUGH OSLO TORP AIRPORT" (22 Oct 2026)
- "RYANAIR GRANTED PLANNING APPROVAL FOR €50M EXPANSION" (22 Oct 2026)
- "RYANAIR CALLS ON GOVT TO FINALLY ABOLISH DUBLIN AIRPORT CAP FOLLOWING ECJ RULING" (15 Oct 2026)

How the [Oslo Torp release](https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/ryanair-marks-15-million-passengers-through-oslo-torp-airport/) is built: the all-caps headline states a milestone; four short paragraphs give the milestone, the history and local jobs, this season's routes and frequencies, and a new route with its fare; then a quote from a named Ryanair spokesperson and one from the airport. The [Dublin cap release](https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/ryanair-calls-on-govt-to-finally-abolish-dublin-airport-cap-following-ecj-ruling/) states a demand in the headline, argues it in three paragraphs, quotes the CEO in pointed language, and closes with "ENDS".

## Post

Patterns observed on [Ryanair's LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/ryanair) on 22 October 2026, paraphrased in [public_post_examples.md](public_post_examples.md), not verbatim:

- A winter-schedule post opens with an all-caps headline, then stacks investment, low-fare seats, routes, aircraft and jobs as scannable lines.
- An event-travel post lists dates, airport pairs, the reason to travel and a booking call to action.
- A student visit to Ryanair HQ uses a warm welcome, a few emoji, training details and an employer-oriented close.
- An ATC-delay post uses an all-caps headline, a warning emoji, a pointed operational criticism and a call for accountability.
