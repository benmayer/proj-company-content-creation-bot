# Public post observation log

## Observation method

This is a pattern log, not a complete archive. The public examples were reviewed on 22 October 2026 through Ryanair’s public LinkedIn page, the Ryanair Press Team account on X, and the official social-account directory. Social posts can be edited, deleted, or reordered, so every observation keeps its source link and should be rechecked before reuse.

## LinkedIn examples

The public LinkedIn feed shows several recurring formats:

| Observed post | Pattern to reuse | Content constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Morocco winter-schedule post leads with a high-energy all-caps headline, then gives investment, low-fare seats, routes, aircraft, and jobs. | Start with a specific news hook, then stack concrete proof points in a scan-friendly format. | Numbers and superlatives must come from the current source and remain tied to the named market and season. |
| A post about extra flights for Liverpool and Manchester matches lists dates, airport pairs, a travel purpose, and a booking call to action. | Connect an event to a practical travel need and make the itinerary easy to scan. | Do not imply availability outside the listed dates or routes. |
| A student visit to Ryanair HQ uses a warm welcome, emojis, operations/training details, and an employer-oriented closing. | Use a more human and informative tone for people, careers, and community content. | Do not fabricate attendees, job opportunities, visits, or quotes. |
| An ATC-delay post uses an all-caps headline, warning emoji, a pointed operational criticism, and a call for accountability. | Urgent public-interest posts can be direct and opinionated. | Treat criticism and responsibility claims as current, sourced statements requiring human review. |
| A Google Cloud partnership post combines a partnership announcement with operational and technology outcomes, emojis, and a follow-up tease. | Make corporate technology news concrete by explaining what changes for operations or colleagues. | Do not turn a partnership into an unsupported claim about product performance or customer benefit. |

Source for the examples: [Ryanair’s official LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/ryanair). The page is the source of the observations; the table deliberately paraphrases rather than reproduces full posts.

## X / Twitter and Instagram coverage

- [Ryanair Press Team on X](https://x.com/RyanairPress/with_replies) is the public press-office account and is useful for official announcements and company-response language.
- [Ryanair’s official social directory](https://linktr.ee/ryanair) links the public Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook, and YouTube accounts. Use the directory to identify the current account before collecting a post; do not assume that a similarly named account is official.
- The voice guidance in `brand_voice.md` applies across channels, but the examples above are not evidence that every channel uses identical wording. Channel-specific samples should be refreshed before generating a channel-specific draft.

## Reusable content patterns

1. **Route announcement:** place + travel period + new route/frequency + concrete customer benefit + sourced CTA.
2. **Event travel:** event + airport pair + exact dates + booking route.
3. **People/community:** human moment + what happened + why it matters + warm close.
4. **Operational response:** verified issue + direct position + clear customer implication + review before publication.
5. **Corporate update:** partnership or investment + operational consequence + concise proof points.
